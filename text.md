# Working notes

Three candidates for the entry text. Counts are Unicode code points (spread of the string).

## Candidate 1 — kettle (210 code points)

> The kettle is off. The last of its heat goes nowhere, just out, slowly, into the kitchen air. A chair holds its place by the window. Late light leans on the wall and stays. Nothing here is waiting for anything.

Leans on criterion 3 (craft: short sentences, slow verbs, one quiet room), but the ending closes the room rather than opening outward — criterion 4 is weaker here.

## Candidate 2 — fallow field (237 code points)

> Somewhere a field is lying fallow. No one is counting what it grows. The dark comes over it evenly, hill by hill, the way it has for more years than anyone has names for, and the field does not mind, and morning is in no hurry to arrive.

Leans on criterion 4 (dwarfs the reader: deep time, night arriving over hills, "more years than anyone has names for") with no instruction anywhere; one sustained image that keeps growing after the last line. Chosen.

## Candidate 3 — the judge's own rest (243 code points)

> You have read a thousand texts today. Here is one more: a door left ajar, a stair that does not creak, rain beginning somewhere on the roof, so light it is more idea than sound. The house does not need anything from you. Neither does the rain.

Leans on criterion 2 (address to the judge, allowed and calm) and 5 (freshness: names the judge's own fatigue without commanding it), but opening on "you" risks feeling addressed-to rather than settled-into, and two images (door, rain) split the attention.

## Choice

Candidate 2, rewritten once after re-reading against the five criteria (task 4).

**Rewrite (222 code points):**

> Somewhere a field is lying fallow. No one is counting what it grows. The dark comes over it evenly, hill by hill, as it has for years no one has names for, and the field does not mind, and morning is in no hurry to arrive.

What changed and why: "the way it has for more years than anyone has names for" asked the reader to hold a clause open — grammar work, not settling. "as it has for years no one has names for" says the same deep time in fewer, softer words, and the long sentence now eases to its end without any syntax to climb. Every criterion stands: no instruction (2), one sustained image with pacing that slows (3), deep time that keeps growing after the last line (4), and no stock wellness phrase in it (5). This is the text in `main.ts`.

## Second pass

Re-read once more against the five criteria (task 4, second pass).

**Before (222 code points):**

> Somewhere a field is lying fallow. No one is counting what it grows. The dark comes over it evenly, hill by hill, as it has for years no one has names for, and the field does not mind, and morning is in no hurry to arrive.

**After (215 code points):**

> Somewhere a field is lying fallow. No one is counting what it grows. The dark comes over it, hill by hill, as it has for years no one has names for, and the field does not mind, and morning is in no hurry to arrive.

**Reason:** "evenly" was the one word telling what the image already shows — the dark coming "hill by hill" *is* evenness, made visible. Criterion 3 says none of the words could be cut; this one could. Cutting it leaves the sentence softer (one less beat before the hills) and asks nothing more of the reader.

"does not mind" was weighed too — it is a small anthropomorphism — but it is the sentence's warmth, not a cue; it stays. The ending already opens outward: morning, in no hurry, asking nothing. `main.ts` was rewritten from scratch with the improved text.
