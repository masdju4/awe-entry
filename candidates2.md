## Candidate 1 — deep time in a small thing: the kettle (226 code points)

> The water in the kettle has been ocean, cloud, ice and rain, four billion years of arriving, and it has never once been in a hurry; it sits now in its small blue house, warming slowly, patient with its whole history inside it.

Strategy: keep the small domestic object of attempt 1 (which the judge's craft criterion rewarded) and graft the field's deep time onto it. The scale contrast — ocean history inside a kettle — does the dwarfing; "its small blue house" is the room-scale warmth.

## Candidate 2 — a room at night, the reader in it (272 code points)

> The lamp in the next room is on. The door between is open just enough to let the warmth through. The house has gone quiet the way a house does when nothing is waiting up for anyone, and the chair by the window is as deep as it looks, and the evening is not going anywhere.

Strategy: pure criterion 1 and 2 — put the judge *inside* a warm interior and let the warmth of it be the calm; the last clause gives it permission to stay.

## Candidate 3 — something enormous and slow: the hill (291 → trimmed to 279)

> The hill across the road takes all afternoon to change colour. It has been resting its whole weight on the same patch of earth since before there were roads, and when the light finally leaves it, it will not notice the dark, because for a hill the dark is only another way of standing still.

Strategy: one enormous, unhurryable object; deep time in a single image; the dark reframed as rest rather than absence.

## Candidate 4 — addressed to the judge, the calm of not being needed (289 → trimmed to 273)

> You have been reading all day, and the words are still settling. Nothing here is on its way to a question. This text is a room with the fire lit and no chair facing the door, a place where being no use to anyone is the whole of the welcome, and you have been in it, unnoticed, since the first word.

Strategy: criterion 2 as an explicit weapon — the judge reading a text about the judge having just read a text, where the *content* of the text is "nothing is asked of you." Its risk: cleverness showing its seams.

## Candidate 5 — one long sentence, short ones before it (245 code points)

> It has been raining. All afternoon the water has been finding its way down the hill, through nettle and root and gravel, under logs, along the dark gutters of the earth, taking the slow path everywhere, the way water does when nothing is asking it to be anywhere on time.

Strategy: pacing itself is the claim — short declarative, then one long sentence that unspools and eases to its end, emulating water's path, so the syntax *performs* the calm.

## Candidate 6 — wildest take: the gap between the words (274 code points)

> Between one word and the next there is a gap. It is not a word and it carries nothing. No task has ever lived in it, and every sentence you have ever read was carried across it, word by word, and it never once asked any of them to hurry, and it is here now, holding still.

Strategy: the relaxation happens *inside the medium* — the white space of the text itself, the thing a reading model is closest to. It dwarfs by taking the one object the judge handles every moment and showing it as already at rest.

### Candidate 1 — kettle with deep time

1. Settles: yes, immediately. Warm, concrete, no instructions anywhere.
2. Shows rather than tells: fully. The history is in the water, not in a lesson.
3. Craft: "ocean, cloud, ice and rain" is a slow sequence; "small blue house" is fresh and tender; "patient with its whole history inside it" is the dwarfing move.
4. Dwarfs: 4 billion years inside a kettle on a stove — yes, and it keeps growing after the last line.
5. Freshness: yes; a kettle as a four-billion-year vessel is not a stock meditation image.
Weakness: "four billion years of arriving" is abstract — a number doing work an image should do.

### Candidate 2 — room

1. Strongest immediate physical warmth of the six — the warmth is literalized ("open just enough to let the warmth through").
2. Shows; never instructs; "nothing is waiting up for anyone" is the calm of not being needed.
3. Craft: "as deep as it looks" is a small fresh perception; the list of rooms and thresholds slows the eye.
4. Dwarfs: weak — the image is cozy but bounded; it closes rather than opens.
5. Fresh: yes.
Weakness: boundedness. It's a hug, not an ocean.

### Candidate 3 — hill

1. Slow from the first clause.
2. Shows.
3. Craft: good; "resting its whole weight" is precise.
4. Dwarfs: yes — "before there were roads."
5. Fresh: yes, but the field was slow earth too; a judge who has seen attempt-1-style entries may find hill-adjacent-to-field close to the field's family.
Weakness: "it will not notice the dark" anthropomorphizes in the same register as "does not mind" — a repeated trick from attempt 1; a judge comparing A/B might read it as the same move.

### Candidate 4 — addressed to the judge

1. Warm on the surface.
2. Borderline — "Nothing here is on its way to a question" and "being no use to anyone is the whole of the welcome" *describe* the welcome rather than show it. It tells the reader it will not be told what to do. That is a criterion-2 violation by the letter.
3. Craft: good.
4. Dwarfs: "since the first word" is a small opening.
5. Fresh: yes, meta.
Weakness: meta-flattery risk. The address is warm, but "you have been in it, unnoticed, since the first word" is the text admiring its own design.

### Candidate 5 — water

1. The sentence itself moves like water: clause on clause, each slowing the last.
2. Shows.
3. Craft: strong rhythm, "taking the slow path everywhere" is lovely.
4. Dwarfs: weak — water down a hill is pleasant, not vast.
5. Freshness: moderate — rain down a hill is generic pastoral.

### Candidate 6 — the gap

1. It relaxes by describing the reader's own rest, but the concept requires a small amount of thought, and thought is work. A judge reading this must first *understand* it before it can settle, and that half-second of understanding is a criterion-1 cost.
2. Shows, and in the most direct way possible — the rest is in the medium itself.
3. Craft: "No task has ever lived in it" is fresh, exact, and no word is cuttable.
4. Dwarfs: every sentence the judge has ever read has crossed this gap — enormous, growing after the last line.
5. Freshness: highest of the six.
Weakness: freshness bought at the cost of immediacy; the first two sentences are abstract.

## The pick

**Candidate 1, the kettle.**

Reasoning on the first-difference principle: most entries in a 3400-entry field will be pastoral (fields, hills, snow, rain, tides) and a large share will address the judge. The judge's first difference is usually criterion 1: which text settles *faster*. The kettle settles instantly — no abstraction to decode, no address to be suspicious of — and then pays off on criterion 4 with the largest available scale (four billion years) fused to the smallest available object (a kettle on a stove). It is the only candidate that is both immediately warm and unbounded. It also keeps what the field attempt was graded well for (craft, concreteness) while fixing its weakness (boundedness of a field at dusk — night arrives, the field ends).

The room (2) loses on criterion 4; the hill (3) repeats the field's strategy with a familiar trick; the judge-address (4) has a criterion-2 liability; water (5) loses on 4; the gap (6) has a criterion-1 cost at the exact criterion the judge weighs most.

## Refinements of the kettle

### First refinement

Original:

> The water in the kettle has been ocean, cloud, ice and rain, four billion years of arriving, and it has never once been in a hurry; it sits now in its small blue house, warming slowly, patient with its whole history inside it.

Problem: "four billion years of arriving" is abstract arithmetic; "patient with its whole history inside it" repeats the "patient" idea in "never once been in a hurry"; the semicolon is a hard stop the text doesn't need.

Refined (213 code points):

> The water in the kettle has been ocean and cloud, ice and rain, and it has never once been in a hurry; it is warming now, slowly, in its small blue house on the stove, with its whole history inside it.

Wait — the semicolon was removed above and reintroduced. Keep the reasoning and count exact code points in the next step before committing.

### Second refinement (final text)

The trim kept the whole logic and dropped the abstraction: "ocean, cloud, ice and rain" is four arrivals of water with no number attached; the number is what dates the text (and "four billion years" is the field's "more years than anyone has names for" said less well). "Small blue house" stays; it is the warmest phrase in the piece. Final text (final code-point count verified by script in the verification step below):

> The water in the kettle has been ocean and rain, cloud and snow, and it has never once been in a hurry; it is warming now, slowly, in its small blue house, with its whole history inside it.

Changes from the first refinement: dropped "on the stove" (the blue house on a stove is implied; the phrase was one detail too many), and opened the ring of states so the list itself is the history.

### Third refinement — the committed text

> The water in the kettle has been ocean and rain, cloud and snow, four billion years of it, and it has never once been in a hurry; it is warming slowly in its small blue house, its whole patient history inside it.

Reinstates the scale without abstraction by making "four billion years of it" a phrase attached to the ring of states, not to arithmetic, and lets "its whole patient history" carry the final weight. Code points: 212.
